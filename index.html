A simple way to study

1. Learn

Read one small topic and write down the key ideas in your own words.

2. Practise

Answer questions without looking at the notes. Try to explain how you got the answer.

3. Check

Mark your work, correct mistakes and try the tricky questions again tomorrow.

➗ Maths

Use these as a checklist. Your class may cover topics in a different order.

Number skills
  • Order of operations (BIDMAS/BODMAS)
  • Positive and negative numbers
  • Factors, multiples and prime numbers
  • Fractions, decimals and percentages
Percentages and ratio
  • Find a percentage of an amount
  • Percentage increase and decrease
  • Simplify ratios
  • Share amounts in a ratio
Algebra
  • Collect like terms
  • Substitute values into expressions
  • Solve one-step and two-step equations
  • Recognise patterns and sequences
Geometry and measurement
  • Angles on a line and around a point
  • Triangles and quadrilaterals
  • Perimeter, area and volume
  • Units and conversions
Statistics and probability
  • Mean, median, mode and range
  • Read tables and graphs
  • Probability from 0 to 1 or 0% to 100%
  • Experimental vs theoretical probability

✍️ English

Reading comprehension
  • Find the main idea and supporting details
  • Make inferences using clues from the text
  • Explain how evidence supports an answer
  • Identify purpose and audience
Language features
  • Simile and metaphor
  • Personification and onomatopoeia
  • Alliteration and emotive language
  • Facts, opinions, statistics and rhetorical questions
  • AFOREST persuasive techniques
Writing
  • Use a clear introduction, paragraphs and conclusion
  • Use topic sentences and examples
  • Choose words for a particular audience
  • Check punctuation, spelling and sentence variety
Analysing a text
  • What is the writer trying to make readers think or feel?
  • Which language feature is used?
  • What effect does it have, and why?
  • Support your answer with a short example

English practice quiz

Useful paragraph structure

Point: make your main idea clear. Evidence: use an example or quote from the text. Explain: explain how the evidence supports your point. For analysis, also explain the effect on the reader.

🌏 Social Studies

History and evidence
  • Put events in chronological order
  • Explain causes and consequences
  • Identify perspectives and bias
  • Use primary and secondary sources
  • Support claims with evidence
Aotearoa New Zealand
  • Te Tiriti o Waitangi / Treaty of Waitangi: context and different understandings
  • Māori perspectives, iwi and hapū
  • Migration and how communities change
  • Important events and people studied in class
Geography
  • Read maps, keys, scale and coordinates
  • Human and natural features
  • How people interact with environments
  • Causes and effects of environmental issues
Culture and citizenship
  • Culture, identity and belonging
  • Rights, responsibilities and fairness
  • Different viewpoints on an issue
  • How communities make decisions

For Social Studies tests, learn the key terms, know the important examples from class, and practise explaining why events or issues matter.

  • Solids, liquids and gases

Physics
  • Forces and motion

Earth and space
  • Earth's structure and natural processes
  • Weather, climate and the water cycle
  • Solar system and day/night
  • How to read scientific diagrams and data
Scientific skills
  • Identify variables in an investigation
  • Write a prediction and method
  • Record results in a table or graph
  • Make a conclusion using evidence
